SECTION A: 100% SURE TOPICS (SOLVED)

Q1. PATA vs SATA (Hard Disk Interfaces) 100% Sure

Answer:
  • Technology: PATA (Parallel ATA) uses parallel transmission (slower, interference prone), while SATA (Serial ATA) uses serial transmission (faster, efficient).
  • Cabling: PATA uses wide 40/80-pin ribbon cables blocking airflow. SATA uses thin 7-pin cables improving cooling.
  • Configuration: PATA requires Master/Slave jumpers. SATA is Point-to-Point (Plug and Play).
  • Speed: PATA maxes at 133 MB/s. SATA III supports up to 6 Gbps.

Q2. Differentiate between AT and ATX Motherboards. Most Likely

Answer:
  • Power Management: AT uses a physical switch (no software shutdown). ATX uses "Soft Power" allowing the OS to shut down the PC.
  • Design: ATX is rotated 90 degrees to clear space for full-length cards and places the CPU near the fan for better cooling.
  • Connectors: AT had messy cables for ports. ATX has integrated I/O ports on the rear panel.

Q3. Explain the working of a UPS. Types? Important

Answer: A UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply) provides battery backup when mains power fails.
Types: 1. Offline: Switches to battery only when power cuts (5ms delay). 2. Line-Interactive: Uses AVR to fix voltage fluctuations without switching to battery. 3. Online: Always runs off battery (Zero transfer time). Best for servers.
